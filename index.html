01
What is SICA?

Say your friend went to Tahoe over break. You ask, “how was it?” He says, “It was good.” Conversation dead.

SICA teaches the other version: you put your phone down, actually face him, make eye contact. Instead of moving on, you dig one layer deeper — “what’d you end up doing there?” He mentions something specific. You remember it, and bring it up the next time you see him.

That’s it. That’s the whole skill. It’s what makes someone feel like you’re actually curious about them, not just being polite — and that’s the difference between a real connection and a surface one.

People call this communication and emotional/social intelligence — which really just means staying level-headed in the moment, and actually paying attention to the person in front of you. Small skills like this one are what SICA is built around.

Which skills will be practiced?
  • Remembering someone’s name after meeting them once
  • Noticing when someone’s checked out of a conversation before they say it
  • Asking one real follow-up instead of just saying “cool”
  • Joining a conversation already in progress without it being awkward
  • Knowing what to say when a room goes quiet
  • Disagreeing with someone without it turning into a fight
  • Making a good first impression in the first few seconds
  • Reading someone’s body language before they say a word
  • Getting a group project actually moving instead of stalling
  • Turning small talk into an actual conversation

So how do you actually teach that?
01Learn10 min

One useful idea: ask one follow-up before changing the subject.

02Practice25 min

You pair up. One person shares something small — what they did this weekend. Instead of a flat “oh nice,” the other person faces them, makes eye contact, asks one real follow-up. That’s the entire rep.

Awkward→Better
Quiet→Speaking up
Rambling→Clear
Defensive→Curious
Stranger→Friend
03Feedback

Your partner gives you one small thing to keep and one to change.

What worked

Who built this?

Not just heard. Listened to.

As Vice President of Codestarters, I watched something strange happen over and over: the students who got picked for leadership roles — and who actually performed once they had them — weren’t always the ones with the best grades. It came down to communication. The ability to give your ideas real weight, to influence a room instead of getting talked over.

That’s why I built SICA: a club that teaches you to be listened to. Not just heard — listened to.
