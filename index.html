Influence isn't popularity.

Popularity is how many people know you. Influence is being able to say what you mean, understand other people, and move a conversation forward.

It is not about being the loudest person in the room. It is about knowing how to listen, explain an idea, and show up with confidence. Those are learnable skills.

What a meeting looks like

Not a lecture. A practice room.

You don't learn communication by hearing about it. You learn it by doing it, getting feedback, and doing it again. Here's an actual meeting, not a framework.

Meeting 5 — Active Listening
The idea — 10 min

There are three levels of listening. Almost everyone operates at level one — waiting for their turn to talk.

The practice — 25 min

You pair up. Your partner talks for three minutes about something that actually matters to them. You can't speak — not one word. Then you repeat back what they said, including what they didn't say out loud.

The challenge — this week

Have one conversation where you ask five questions and make zero statements about yourself.

You're never put on a stage. You're never called on cold. Everything starts in pairs.

04
Where it shows up

More than a skill for the classroom.

The skills travel with you: to a new friend’s lunch table, an interview, a team meeting, a presentation, or the conversation you have been putting off.

You do not need to become a different person. You need more ways to express the person you already are—especially when you feel nervous, rushed, or out of place.

The goal is simple: leave with a set of habits you can use anywhere. Not a line for a résumé. A change you can feel.

05
Why SICA

Not another speech club.

You will not spend meetings memorizing speeches or competing to sound the smartest. You will practice the conversations that happen before, after, and between the big moments.

That means room for the student who already loves the stage and the student who would rather begin in a pair. Everyone gets repetitions.

It is a place to become more clear, calm, and capable with people.

06
The cost of skipping this

It doesn't show up on a report card.

Look at the founders and the dropouts who actually made it. It wasn't because their idea was the smartest one in the room — it was because they connected with the right people, built real networks, and carried a presence other people could actually feel.

Name any profession — doctor, engineer, founder, lawyer, athlete, executive. The pattern holds every time. SICA shows up in every single one.

Here's what nobody tells you: coasting through high school with good grades and zero communication practice doesn't catch up with you senior year.

It catches up at 22 — across the table from someone who spent three years learning to read a room, while you spent zero hours.

The gap never shows on a report card. It shows up the first time it costs you something real: the internship, the offer, the room where the decision about you gets made.

Founding story

I built a club that teaches you to be listened to.

As Vice President of Codestarters, I watched it happen constantly — who got picked for leadership, who actually performed once they had it. Grades had almost nothing to do with it. It came down to communication: the ability to give your ideas real weight, to influence a room instead of getting talked over.

So I built a club that teaches you to be listened to. Not just heard.

The kind of presence where every time you speak, people think, “This person can actually get things done.”

That's the power I want every SICA student to have.
