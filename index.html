Have you ever…

You had something worth saying… but stayed quiet.

You thought of the perfect response… after the conversation ended.

You wanted to introduce yourself… but talked yourself out of it.

You knew the answer… but never raised your hand.

Those moments happen to everyone.

They aren’t personality traits.They’re skills.

If you’ve experienced even one of those, you’re exactly who SICA was built for.

01
What this actually is

This isn’t public speaking.

Think about a group project. One person naturally gets everyone talking — they listen, keep things on track, get people to agree, and lead. Someone else in that same group is quiet, maybe even the smartest person there, but never says much.

That gap isn’t talent. It’s a skill nobody ever taught either of them. SICA is about that gap — everyday conversations, not stages or speeches.

02
Why we built it

Grades had almost nothing to do with it.

As Vice President of Codestarters, I watched it happen constantly: who got picked for leadership roles, who actually performed once they had them — and grades had almost nothing to do with it. It came down to communication. The ability to give your ideas real weight, to influence a room instead of getting talked over.

That’s why I built SICA: a club that teaches you to be listened to. Not just heard — listened to.

Founder · Cupertino High School

Why this matters

Even in the fields you’d least expect.

Grades matter. Technical skills matter. People skills matter too.

Research on engineering careers found that up to 70% of career advancement traced back to communication skills, not technical output.

Nearly 90% of employers screen for problem-solving and nearly 80% for teamwork — while reliance on GPA as a screening tool has fallen below 40% and keeps dropping. Sources: NACE Job Outlook 2024–2025; research on engineering career advancement.

These aren’t just school skills. They’re the ones that actually decide outcomes.
